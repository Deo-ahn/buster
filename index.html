01 / OVERVIEW

Scout continuously.
Burst selectively.

Long videos need continuous attention. They do not need a large vision-language model on every frame.

BUSTER is a label-free framework that distills a vision-language model (VLM) into a lightweight recurrent student. The student learns both anomaly scores and when to inspect the stream more densely, using a Scout-and-Burst sampling policy.

Semantic pseudo-labels from a Qwen2.5-VL-32B teacher guide training without manual anomaly annotations. A rank-aware objective teaches the student to separate confident anomalous and normal moments. At inference, the student monitors the stream at low frame rate, revisits suspicious intervals at higher resolution, and reserves expensive VLM reasoning for selected burst segments.

Across UCF-Crime, XD-Violence, and ShanghaiTech, the paper reports strong unsupervised detection performance. On UCF-Crime, BUSTER matches dense 8 FPS detection while processing 53.2% of the frames.

02 / METHOD

From a teacher’s knowledge
to a student’s attention.

Learn offline. Monitor efficiently.
Explain on demand.

01OFFLINE

Mine the teacher

Query Qwen2.5-VL-32B with overlapping 2-second windows at 4 FPS. Normalize the Yes/No answer probabilities into soft anomaly targets.

“Does any anomaly event occur in this video? Answer Yes or No.”
02LIGHTWEIGHT STUDENT

Learn when to look

Combine frozen LanguageBind features with their temporal differences. Train a recurrent student using score matching, ranking, and temporal smoothness.

ℒ = ℒdist + λrankℒrank + λsmoothℒsmooth
03ON DEMAND

Reason on bursts

Switch from 4 to 8 FPS around high-score intervals. Merge candidates, allocate a bounded frame budget, and ask the VLM for segment-level explanations.

05 / LIMITATIONS

Where Scout-and-Burst
still falls short.

Better temporal evidence does not remove every perceptual or reasoning error.

01

Small, brief, or occluded events

A short action involving a small target can remain difficult for the teacher to perceive, even when the surrounding interval is available.

02

Consequences versus causes

An explanation may focus on a visible aftermath instead of the decisive action. Detecting an anomaly does not guarantee a correct causal account.

03

Domain and category ambiguity

Teacher priors and domain shifts affect results. A visually grounded description can also differ from a dataset’s specific event category.
